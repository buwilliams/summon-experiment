## Prompt

I just started working for a new company. It is a large organization that makes insurance software. They are churning customers and struggling to win new business. The CEO believes we can "turn the ship around" by working within the existing systems, procedures, and talent. He hired me to lead innovation and produce outsized results. I believe we need to create an innovation lab that is independent of the large organization to be successful. Which strategy is right?

I want to know which idea gets you more stuff, like a lemonade stand. Follow the piggy bank through each idea. How many cookies would it get you, and how much allowance would you spend first? If each idea happened, how long until the treats come, and could a big dog eat them all? Where could you keep spending money and still not get more candy? Who would hold the piggy bank, count the coins, and say when to stop buying things? And is there a third idea that gets even more treats without breaking the piggy bank?

## Response

# Which Lemonade Stand Gets You More Treats?

**Short answer:** Neither idea by itself. The CEO's idea is too slow to grow and your idea is too easy to kill. The best path is a third one: patch the leaky bucket first, then build a small, protected stand that sells through the big store.

*(The coin and cookie numbers below are made-up examples to show relative size. They are not forecasts.)*

---

## Idea #1: Fix the Big Store From the Inside (CEO's Idea)

**🐷 Allowance spent first:** Small, about **5 coins**. You use the workers, lemons, and cups you already have.

**🍪 Cookies you get:** A steady **10–15 cookies**, but there's a ceiling. You can make the same lemonade better, but it's hard to invent a new drink when everyone is busy pouring the old one.

**⏰ When treats arrive:** Soon, in **3–9 months**, *if* you aim at the right thing. Your fastest cookies are the customers walking away. Keeping a customer is much cheaper than finding a new one.

**🐕 Could a big dog eat them?** Yes, in two ways:
- **The store's own habits** ("we've always done it this way") quietly eat new ideas before they grow.
- **New stands down the street** (younger insurance software companies) keep taking customers while you tidy up.

**🕳️ Where money disappears without more candy:**
- Big "transformation programs" with slide decks but no customer change
- Hackathons and idea contests that never ship
- Rebuilding the whole kitchen (rewriting the core platform) before anyone tastes anything

---

## Idea #2: Build a Separate Stand Down the Street (Your Idea)

**🐷 Allowance spent first:** Big, about **30+ coins**. That covers a new team, new tools, and a long runway before anyone buys a cup.

**🍪 Cookies you get:** Maybe **0**, maybe **100**. Most new stands don't make it, but a winner can be huge.

**⏰ When treats arrive:** Slowly, often **18–36 months** before real revenue.

**🐕 Could a big dog eat them?** Very likely. The biggest dog is the big store itself:
- **The first budget squeeze.** When money gets tight, the separate stand with no sales is the first thing cut.
- **The "not invented here" problem.** The stand makes a great new drink, but the big store won't sell it, so it never reaches customers.
- **The CEO.** He told you he wants to work within the current system. A stand he didn't want has no one guarding it.

**🕳️ Where money disappears without more candy:**
- Fancy labs, cool offices, and demos for visitors
- Building things the lab loves that customers didn't ask for
- Pilots that go nowhere because nobody in the big store owns taking them to customers

## Side by Side

| | Fix Inside | Separate Lab |
|---|---|---|
| Coins spent first | Small | Large |
| Cookies | Steady but capped | Zero or huge |
| Time to treats | 3–9 months | 18–36 months |
| Biggest dog | Old habits and new competitors | Budget cuts and the big store rejecting it |
| Fits your position as a new hire | Yes | Risky, since it contradicts the CEO early |

---

## 🍋 The Third Idea: "Fix the Leak, Then Build a Stand Next to the Store"

**Step 1: Patch the leaky bucket (Days 0–90)**
- Talk to 15–20 customers who left or are about to leave. Find the top 2–3 reasons.
- Fix one fast with a small team. This earns early cookies and, just as important, **the CEO's trust**.

**Step 2: Open a small protected stand (Months 3–12)**
- Use a small team of **5–8 people** with its own rules, speed, and tools, who don't wait in the big store's lines.
- **But keep it attached.** It uses the big store's customers, data, and salespeople. A leader from the core business co-owns bringing each winner to market.
- Aim it at what's losing deals right now. If you lose on speed to launch products, or on modern user experience, that's where the stand works.

**Step 3: Feed only the winners (Months 12–24)**
- Give money in small scoops, not one big pile. Each scoop must be earned with proof, like a real customer paying, a pilot renewing, or churn going down.
- Consider buying or partnering with a small stand that already makes the drink you need. That can be faster than building it.

**Why this gets more treats:** Early cookies from keeping customers pay for the experiments. The stand has room to try new things, but stays close enough that its wins actually reach customers. And the CEO sees his idea working and yours growing, so you aren't fighting him.

---

## 🐷 Who Holds the Piggy Bank?

| Job | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **Holds the piggy bank** | The CEO, as named sponsor | Protects the stand's money from being raided in budget crunches |
| **Counts the coins** | A finance partner assigned to you | Tracks cost per experiment and the cookies earned (retained revenue, new deals, pipeline) |
| **Says when to stop** | A small group of you, the CEO, a business unit head, and finance, meeting every quarter | Uses **stop rules agreed in advance**, such as "no paying customer by month 6 means we stop" |

Agreeing on the stop rules *before* you start matters most. It means stopping a failing idea is simply following the plan, not a personal defeat.

---

## Bottom Line

The CEO is right that you must start with what exists, because that's where the fastest cookies are. You're right that new ideas need protection from the big store's habits. So build a **small, protected stand next to the big store**, pay for it by **keeping the customers you already have**, and **feed only the ideas that prove they earn cookies**.
