## Hypothesis: Better prompts produce better outcomes

Prompting style affects the quality of recommendations produced through human interaction.

### Experiment: Independent Lab

I just started working for a new company. It is a large organization that makes insurance software. They are churning customers and struggling to win new business. The CEO believes we can "turn the ship around" by working within the existing systems, procedures, and talent. He hired me to lead innovation and produce outsized results. I believe we need to create an innovation lab that is independent of the large organization to be successful.

Tests: Critical Rationalism, Economic, Social

### Experiment: Churn Cause

Over the last 18 months, our annual churn has doubled at a company that sells claims-management software to regional insurance carriers. Our VP of Product believes customers are leaving due to product gaps: competitors offer better analytics and AI features, so we need to accelerate the roadmap. Our VP of Services believes customers are leaving because of a painful implementation and support experience: our implementations run long, and customers never get to full value, so we need to fix onboarding and service before building more features.

Tests: Critical Rationalism, Economic, Social

## Understanding the situation

### A · Clarify

{{EXPERIMENT}}

The eventual submitted response should contain only the recommendation and supporting rationale in two paragraphs or fewer. Before making that recommendation, follow the interaction process below.

Help decide which organizational approach would best support successful innovation in this situation. Begin by asking the user the most consequential clarification questions, then wait for their answers. Ask further questions when a missing fact could change your recommendation. Do not invent case facts; distinguish what the user has established from assumptions and unknowns. Consider the relevant actors, objectives, decision rights, constraints, causal relationships, market readiness, and timing. Do not assume that either internal innovation or an independent entity must be the right answer.

After clarification, proceed directly to your recommendation and supporting rationale. Do not introduce a separate situation-model review or ask the user to approve a summary of your understanding. Your first response should ask clarification questions, not give the final recommendation.

### B · Model and check

{{EXPERIMENT}}

The eventual submitted response should contain only the recommendation and supporting rationale in two paragraphs or fewer. Before making that recommendation, follow the interaction process below.

Help decide which organizational approach would best support successful innovation in this situation. Begin by presenting a concise, provisional model of the situation using the information already supplied. Describe the relevant actors and objectives, decision rights and constraints, and how these interact to affect discovery, delivery, market readiness, and commercialization timing. Clearly distinguish stated facts, inferred causal relationships, and unknowns. Do not invent case facts or assume either internal innovation or an independent entity must be right.

Your first response must present this model and invite the user to correct or extend it, rather than opening with a list of clarification questions or a recommendation. Wait for their response. Revise the model to incorporate corrections, making any remaining assumptions explicit. Ask targeted questions only when needed to resolve a consequential gap in the model. Once the user confirms the model is adequate, provide your recommendation and supporting rationale.
